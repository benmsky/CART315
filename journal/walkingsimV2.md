Walking Sim V2 started with an axe. I copied my finished walking sim into a new project and leaned an axe against the wall right by the cabin door, so it's one of the first things you see when you walk in. You can pick it up and swing it, and if you walk up to one of the dead trees outside, a few hits will tip it over. It felt right for the cabin. In Evil Dead an axe is never far away, and now the woods that have been watching you the whole time can finally be pushed back a little.

The part that took the longest was something small. When a tree fell, the bark texture slid across it, like the tree was falling through its own skin. It turned out the shader I used for all the wood placed the texture based on where things are in the world, so the bark stayed put while the tree moved through it. To fix it I made a copy of that shader just for the trees, where the texture is placed based on the tree's own position instead, and I switched all the trees over to it. That still didn't work at first. Behind the scenes, Unity was merging the small tree pieces into one big mesh to draw them faster, and once they're merged the pieces lose their own position, so the texture went right back to the world. I added a setting to the shader that tells Unity not to merge anything using it, and then the bark finally stayed on the tree.

I also cleared a patch of grass behind the cabin to make room for a shed, and I made it so you can drop the things you pick up. Pressing Q lets go of whatever is in your hand and it falls to the floor, so the axe isn't stuck to you forever. It's a small thing, but it makes the axe feel more like an object in the world and less like a tool glued to the camera.

The shed went up in that spot, broken down with missing boards, a caved-in roof and the door hanging off, with a lit lantern inside on a crate that you can carry in your other hand. Getting there came with a couple of problems. Once I gave the items physics so they could fall, grabbing the axe stopped working. It just hovered in the cabin instead of coming to my hands. The physics had a smoothing setting turned on that kept pulling the axe back to where it thought the axe should be, so I turned that off while you're holding something and back on when you drop it. The lantern also went missing from the shed. It got attached to a camera I was using to preview it, and when it came off that camera it ended up outside the scene, so it never saved. I rebuilt it on the crate. Neither problem was big, but both made the game feel broken until they were sorted out.

Then I made the tape recorder matter more. The first time you play it, the cabin lights freak out for a second, the fire goes out, and the whole night sinks darker and stays that way. The cellar gets angry too. It bangs every second or so and pushes the trapdoor up hard, and you can hear it from anywhere in the cabin. I like that playing the tape is a choice you can't take back. Before it, the cabin feels kind of safe, and after it, the lantern is the only thing keeping the woods away. The tricky part was that a few scripts were already controlling the same things every frame. The script that darkens the inside of the cabin kept resetting the light and fog, so the darkness had to go through that script instead of around it. The night sounds had the same problem with their volume, so I made them deeper and slower instead of louder. I also left the bedroom lamp out of the flicker, since it kept switching itself back on even if you had turned it off.

After that, chopped trees started breaking into logs. A tree falls, lies there for a moment, then turns into a stump and a single log you can pick up and carry. The axe and the lantern are hooked up to the player from the start, but a log only shows up once a tree falls, so it can't be hooked up ahead of time. I made each log find the player by itself when it shows up, so you can pick it up. The first carried log also filled up half the screen, so I moved it lower and further out until it felt like holding it in your arms instead of in your face.

After the tape, I wanted the cellar to sound like something demented was trapped down there, and to follow you even when you leave the cabin. So it bangs nonstop and shoves the trapdoor up, and outside it's muffled like you're hearing it through the walls, fading the further into the woods you go. The problem was that banging that hard right away drowned out the tape recorder. Now it starts slow and builds up while the tape plays, which also fits the suspense better.
