In Chapter 2, "Rules: Change Comes From Within," Pippin Barr talks about how a game’s code and rules aren't set in stone, but are actually just materials you can play around with and change. What really stuck with me was how making even the smallest tweak or keeping a totally accidental glitch can completely change the vibe and feeling of the whole thing.
A lot of my own work revolves around collage, so this idea really resonated with me. When I’m working on a piece, I’m usually taking established elements and seeing how tweaking them changes the bigger picture. Moving a single object or changing the shading might seem minor, but it can completely reconstruct the entire image. Barr’s Pong experiments do the exact same thing. By making simple code tweaks, like taking away collision in Ghost Pong or hiding the ball in Blind Pong, he completely shifts how you experience the game, turning a basic sport into something strange or isolated.
It actually reminded me of a time I was working on a scene in Unreal Engine and accidentally added fog to the environment. It was supposed to just be a normal, realistic place, but the fog turned it into this mystical, dreamlike space. It totally shifted the atmosphere and felt really nostalgic, so even though it was a complete accident, just like the glitch Barr decided to leave in Fertility Pong, I knew I had to keep it.
Overall, the chapter was a great reminder that rules aren't just rigid boundaries. Whether you're messing with code or digital art, leaning into small tweaks and happy accidents is usually how you get the coolest results.
