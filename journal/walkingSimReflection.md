
For the walking simulator I went with the cabin in the woods from Evil Dead 2. It's one of those places that's instantly recognizable even though it's really just a small log cabin, so I wanted to see if I could get it to read as "that cabin" using only Unity's built-in shapes. Everything is made out of cubes, cylinders and spheres. The walls are stacked cylinders with the corners crossing over each other, there's a triangle-shaped roof, a stone chimney, a front porch with a swing, and an old car parked out front. Inside there's the fireplace, the table with the Book of the Dead and the tape recorder, the mounted deer head, a wall clock, two little bedrooms, and the cellar trapdoor chained shut in the middle of the floor. It's only one floor, which kept it manageable.

Most of my attention went into the atmosphere. You start at the end of a dirt path in a dead forest at night, with thick blue fog and cold moonlight everywhere. The only warm light in the whole scene is the cabin, from the glowing windows, the porch bulb and the fire inside, so you naturally walk toward it without me needing any arrows or UI. The fog also hides the edge of the map, so the forest feels like it keeps going. Sound helps a lot too. There's a spooky forest ambience playing the whole time, you can hear the fire crackling before you get inside, the floor has wooden footsteps, and the porch swing keeps banging against the cabin wall. When you get close to the trapdoor, something starts thumping on it from underneath, and there's red light leaking out of the cracks. That red is the one strong colour left in the scene, so it really stands out.

Right now the scene is pretty quiet and still, and that's just where the project is at. The props are kept to the main pieces, the lights mostly hold steady, and the colours are muted and grey. For now the swing and the trapdoor are only sounds, and I actually like that, because hearing something without seeing it lets your brain fill in the rest. But I see this as a base to build on before I figure out where I want to give the cabin more life, whether that's animation, more detail inside, or more going on in the woods.

I also got really interested in adding clouds. I liked the idea of a moody sky where clouds slowly drift over the moon and block the moonlight, with the light piercing through them a bit. I tried a few versions, including painted clouds with moon rays and moving clouds that dimmed the light as they passed the moon, and I even looked at a tutorial to figure out how to make them better. I just didn't like how any of it turned out. The rays looked cheap, and the clouds either felt too far away or didn't look the way I had pictured them. So I got rid of the clouds completely and went with a heavy fog instead. The moon is now a soft blue glow pushing through the fog, and the moonlight still reaches the ground and throws tree shadows, which gives me the mood I was after with a much simpler idea.

The scripts are pretty simple. LightFlicker changes a light's brightness randomly, SwingBang uses a timer to play the swing hitting the wall every few seconds, and TrapdoorRattle checks how close the player is and plays the thumps. MuffleIndoors checks if the player is inside the cabin and softly muffles the outside sounds, like the forest ambience and the porch swing, so the walls feel like they're really between you and the woods. This is still a rough draft and not a finished piece. The cellar can't be opened and everything is very blocky. Next I'd like to add animation, like the swing actually swinging into the wall and the trapdoor jumping every time something hits it from below, and maybe try something like the evil force camera rushing through the trees, or doors slamming as you walk through the cabin. But the main idea is there.

Since the first draft I’ve gone back into the cabin and given it a lot more life. The biggest change is that you can actually do things now. If you look at the tape recorder on the table and press E, it plays the tape and the reels spin, and pressing E again stops it. The tape only plays inside the cabin, so if you walk out it fades away. I also built out the second bedroom. In the first bedroom there’s a lamp you can switch on and off with a click, and in the second one there’s a curtain you can open and close. When the curtain is closed the room goes dark, and when you open it the moonlight comes back in and you can see the foggy woods through the window.

I also wanted the walk up to the cabin to feel like something is aware of you. When you get close, the front door slowly creaks open by itself. At the same moment the headlights on the old car flicker and die, with a groaning metal sound. I like that these two things happen together, because it feels like the arrival is being noticed. I also moved the cellar trapdoor so it sits under the deer head. Now when you’re near it, something underneath knocks, and every so often it pushes the door up a little before it slams back down. In my first draft the trapdoor and the swing were only sounds. I’ve since made the swing rock just a tiny bit, and I took its banging sound out completely because it was getting kind of annoying.

The look of the scene changed a lot too. The logs, planks and stone have real textures now, there’s 3D grass, the windows are dirty, and the inside has a couch, a rug and a fireplace with actual flames. The forest is also almost twice as thick, so both sides of the path are a wall of dead trees fading into the fog. One thing that bugged me was that the inside of the cabin looked grey and foggy, like the night outside was leaking in. So now when you step inside, the blue night light and the fog get turned down, and the room is mostly lit by the lamp and the fire, which feels a lot warmer. I also fixed the windows, which were kind of glowing on their own.

Sound got reworked as well. I took out the forest ambience from the first draft and replaced it with a scary night ambience that only plays outside, so stepping into the cabin feels like stepping away from the woods. Inside, you hear the fireplace and the wall clock ticking, and the pendulum swings. The floor creaks in a few spots, the door has an old creaking door sound, and the car has its groaning metal. I swapped most of the early placeholder sounds for real recordings, which made a big difference.
