*Rough draft*

For the walking simulator I went with the cabin in the woods from Evil Dead 2. It's one of those places that's instantly recognizable even though it's really just a small log cabin, so I wanted to see if I could get it to read as "that cabin" using only Unity's built-in shapes. Everything is made out of cubes, cylinders and spheres. The walls are stacked cylinders with the corners crossing over each other, there's a gable roof, a stone chimney, a front porch with a swing, and an old car parked out front. Inside there's the fireplace, the table with the Book of the Dead and the tape recorder, the mounted deer head, a wall clock, two little bedrooms, and the cellar trapdoor chained shut in the middle of the floor. It's only one floor, which kept it manageable.

Most of my attention went into the atmosphere. You start at the end of a dirt path in a dead forest at night, with thick blue fog and cold moonlight everywhere. The only warm light in the whole scene is the cabin, from the glowing windows, the porch bulb and the fire inside, so you naturally walk toward it without me needing any arrows or UI. The fog also hides the edge of the map, so the forest feels like it keeps going. Sound helps a lot too. There's wind the whole time, you can hear the fire crackling before you get inside, the floor has wooden footsteps, and the porch swing keeps banging against the cabin wall. When you get close to the trapdoor, something starts thumping on it from underneath, and there's red light leaking out of the cracks. That red is the one strong colour left in the scene, so it really stands out.

At first the scene felt too busy and too alive for a place that's supposed to feel abandoned. I stripped out a lot of the small props, made the lights hold almost steady instead of flickering, and drained the colour out of everything so it looks greyer and deader. I also took the movement away from the swing and the trapdoor but kept their sounds. I think hearing them without seeing anything move is actually creepier, because your brain fills in the rest. It's the same kind of lesson as the fog accident I had in Unreal, where taking something away or changing one small thing completely changed the mood.

The scripts are pretty simple. LightFlicker changes a light's brightness randomly, SwingMotion uses Mathf.Sin to time the swing and play the bang, and TrapdoorRattle checks how close the player is and plays the thumps. Each one has a checkbox so I can turn the movement back on if I change my mind. This is still a rough draft and a prototype more than a finished piece. The cellar can't be opened, everything is very blocky, and I'd like to try something like the evil force camera rushing through the trees, or doors slamming as you walk through the cabin. But the main idea is there, and I can explain every part of it.
